# Medication Safety, Monitoring & Self-Advocacy

PH medications should be started, stopped, combined, or titrated only with guidance from a pulmonary hypertension specialist or other qualified clinician. The right plan depends on the PH type, risk level, symptoms, test results, response to therapy, side effects, and other health conditions.

## Questions worth asking

- What pathway does this medication target?
- What benefit are we looking for, and when might I notice it?
- What side effects need a routine call, and what requires urgent care?
- What bloodwork or other monitoring is required?
- Does this interact with any prescriptions, over-the-counter medicines, supplements, alcohol, or nitrates?
- Does pregnancy, liver disease, kidney disease, HIV treatment, or another condition change how this medication should be used?

Some PH therapies can lower blood pressure or carry medication-specific warnings. Sudden vision or hearing changes, fainting, severe dizziness, chest pain, trouble breathing, allergic symptoms, or other severe reactions require prompt medical attention.

## Track what changes

A simple symptoms-and-activity journal can help you record dose changes, side effects, exercise tolerance, blood pressure or other requested measurements, and questions for your care team.

Mental health is part of whole-person PH care. Ask for support when the emotional weight of treatment and chronic illness becomes difficult to carry alone.
