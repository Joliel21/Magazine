# What Is Pulmonary Hypertension?

Pulmonary hypertension (PH) is a broad term for conditions that cause abnormally high pressure in the blood vessels of the lungs. When those vessels become narrowed, blocked, stiff, or otherwise abnormal, the right side of the heart has to work harder to move blood through the lungs.

PH is not one single disease. Different forms have different causes, treatments, and outlooks. Pulmonary arterial hypertension (PAH) is one type of PH, but the terms PH and PAH are not interchangeable.

## Symptoms can be easy to miss

Common symptoms can include:

- Shortness of breath, especially with activity
- Fatigue or unusual weakness
- Dizziness, lightheadedness, or fainting
- Chest pain or pressure
- Swelling in the ankles, legs, or abdomen
- A racing, pounding, or irregular heartbeat
- Bluish or grayish lips or fingertips when oxygen levels are low

These symptoms can overlap with asthma, heart disease, deconditioning, anemia, lung disease, and many other conditions. That overlap is one reason PH may take time to recognize.

**Awareness matters:** persistent or worsening symptoms deserve medical evaluation, especially when everyday activity becomes harder than it used to be.
