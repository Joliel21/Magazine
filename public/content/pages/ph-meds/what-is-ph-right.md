# How PH Is Diagnosed

There is no single screening test that answers every PH question. Diagnosis usually builds from symptoms, medical history, physical examination, and several tests that help determine whether PH is present and what may be causing it.

A workup may include:

- Echocardiogram
- Electrocardiogram (ECG/EKG)
- Blood tests
- Chest X-ray or CT imaging
- Pulmonary function testing
- Oxygen assessment and, when appropriate, sleep testing
- Ventilation/perfusion (V/Q) scanning or other testing for chronic blood clots
- Six-minute walk testing or other exercise assessment

## Right-heart catheterization

When pulmonary hypertension is suspected, right-heart catheterization is the key test used to directly measure pressures in the heart and pulmonary circulation and to confirm the hemodynamic diagnosis. The results help the care team understand the type and severity of PH and guide treatment decisions.

Because treatment differs substantially among PH groups, evaluation by clinicians with pulmonary hypertension expertise is important.

**Seek urgent medical care** for severe or rapidly worsening shortness of breath, fainting, chest pain, new confusion, blue or gray coloring, or other symptoms that may represent an emergency.
